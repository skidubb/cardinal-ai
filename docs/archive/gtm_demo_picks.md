# GTM Demo Runway — one protocol per orchestration pattern

Six picks. Each is the single highest-value protocol in its orchestration family — chosen on utility, novelness, low theater, and complexity fit — paired with a GTM question engineered to require that pattern's signature move.

Scores are `Utility / Theater / Novelness · Complexity`.

---

## 1. Single → `p25 system_archetype_detection`
**6 / 5 / 7 · complexity 7 · Systems Thinking**

**Question**
> "Our Customer Success org has hired 14 CSMs over six quarters to handle escalations. Each time we staff up, escalations drop for two quarters, then climb right back to the prior level — sometimes higher. NRR is stuck at 104%. We've tried playbooks, QBR cadence changes, a new CS platform. Identify the system archetype at play, the reinforcing and balancing loops, and the single highest-leverage intervention point."

**Why this pairing**
Single-agent orchestration's quiet strength is *pattern match, not debate*. System archetypes (Limits to Growth, Shifting the Burden, Fixes That Fail, Success to the Successful) are a library of stable structures — one well-prompted agent does the recognition work in one pass. The audience sees: *you didn't need five agents. You needed the right frame.* The above scenario is classic **Shifting the Burden** — treating the symptom (escalations) atrophies the capability to fix the root cause (activation/fit defects).

## 2. Sequence → `p32 tetlock_forecast`
**10 / 2 / 9 · complexity 9 · Forecasting**

**Question**
> "By December 31, 2026, will we retain ≥90% of logo count in our top-50 legacy-pricing enterprise accounts, given the forced packaging migration that begins July 1? Produce a calibrated probability. Show your reference class and base rate, inside view from account-level signals, outside view from comparable migrations in SaaS history, a red-team pass on your own assumptions, and a final aggregated forecast with a confidence interval. Name the three leading indicators we should be watching monthly."

**Why this pairing**
Tetlock's superforecasting method is a textbook **sequence**: base rate → inside view → outside view → adversarial critique → aggregation. Each step only becomes possible once the prior step has landed. This question is engineered to require all five stages — a probability without base rates is vibes; an inside view without an outside view is narrative. The U=10 score is earned: this protocol produces the single most decision-grade artifact in the registry.

## 3. Parallel → `p03 parallel_synthesis`
**8 / 3 / 4 · complexity 4 · Integration**

**Question**
> "We need to choose the single highest-leverage move for FY27 that we can actually execute. Run independent 90-second strategic takes from four personas — CFO (margin and capital), CRO (pipeline and productivity), CPO (product-led expansion), CMO (demand and brand) — with no visibility into each other's drafts. Then synthesize: where do at least three of them converge, where do they genuinely conflict, and what decision-right or sequencing resolves each conflict?"

**Why this pairing**
Parallel synthesis's signature move is **non-contamination**. If the personas see each other, you get anchoring and artificial consensus — the exact failure mode of real GTM leadership meetings. Running them truly in parallel produces four independent priors, and the synthesis step makes the disagreement visible rather than papering over it. Demo beat for the audience: *this is what independence actually looks like.*

## 4. Hub-and-spoke → `p17 red_blue_white`
**8 / 4 / 6 · complexity 7 · Adversarial**

**Question**
> "A Tier-1 competitor just announced a Free Forever tier that overlaps ~70% of our Starter SKU feature set, with a credible enterprise upsell path. Run three independent spokes: **Red** — three concrete mechanisms by which they take 25% of our mid-market pipeline in the next 18 months. **Blue** — three defensible moats we can activate in the next 90 days without price-matching. **White** — a neutral probability-weighted read on which Red scenarios are live and which Blue moves actually bind. Hub reconciles into a single recommendation with a kill-criterion."

**Why this pairing**
Hub-and-spoke shines when the spokes must be **mandate-isolated**: Red must not soften, Blue must not concede, White must not pick a side. The hub's reconciliation is where the signal lands. A debate format would contaminate the mandates; sequence would bias by ordering. This is the one shape that fits. GTM audience recognition is instant — Red/Blue/White comes straight from IC tradecraft and every GTM leader has wanted a clean version of it.

## 5. Hybrid → `p38 klein_premortem`
**10 / 2 / 9 · complexity 8 · Risk / Pre-Mortem**

**Question**
> "Assume it is April 2027. Our AI-native PLG motion launched last July and it has clearly failed: PLG-sourced ARR is $1.8M against a $15M plan, activation rate is 11% against a 35% target, and the board is reviewing a wind-down. First in parallel, enumerate the failure stories across product, pricing, activation, expansion, sales/PLG handoff, and brand — one story per lane, no blending. Then in sequence, trace the dominant causal chain across lanes, identify the two pre-conditions most likely to have fired first, and rank the leading indicators we should be watching *now* — today, April 2026 — so we see the failure coming."

**Why this pairing**
Pre-mortem is hybrid by necessity: **parallel enumeration** to avoid anchoring on one story, then **sequenced causal tracing** to find the dominant chain. Either shape alone is worse — parallel-only gives a list, sequence-only gives a single narrative. Klein's method is the gold-standard combination, and it's the protocol GTM leaders will most want to steal for their own use immediately after the talk.

---

## 6. Decentralized → `p53 contract_net`
**7 / 4 / 9 · complexity 6 · Prioritization (task allocation)**

**Question**
> "We just re-segmented the enterprise book — 120 accounts, five AE personas (hunter, farmer, vertical-specialist, technical-closer, strategic-enterprise). No central planner. Each AE independently bids on each account with (a) a win-probability, (b) the capacity cost in hours per week, and (c) a rationale grounded in their past analogous wins. Clear the market: award accounts to the highest expected-pipeline bids, subject to per-AE capacity and a vertical-coverage constraint. Surface the five accounts where no AE bid above threshold — those are the coverage gaps."

**Why this pairing**
Contract-net (Reid Smith, 1980) is the foundational decentralized-coordination protocol: **announce, bid, award**. Territory allocation is the GTM problem that most obviously wants this shape — each AE has private information (past win patterns, relationships, capacity) that a central planner can't see. Any central model here is inferior to letting the agents bid. N=9 earns its keep: nothing else in the registry allocates resources this way. The "coverage gap" emergent output is the magic — decentralized systems *tell you what they can't do*.

## Running order suggestion

1. **Parallel** (`p03`) — warm the room with the most familiar shape; show how real independence produces real disagreement.
2. **Single** (`p25`) — subvert the expectation that more agents are always better; one frame dissolves the knot.
3. **Hub-and-spoke** (`p17`) — Red/Blue/White lands immediately; high energy.
4. **Sequence** (`p32`) — slow down for the calibrated-forecast demo; this is where decision-makers lean in.
5. **Hybrid** (`p38`) — the pre-mortem is the crowd-pleaser; audience will want to run this on their own business.
6. **Decentralized** (`p53`) — close on the most novel shape; territory allocation demo is tangible and memorable.

# Context dossiers — what a real business actually brings

The questions above are the *prompts*. But a real GTM org never shows up with just a question — they show up with a pile of dashboards, CSVs, memos, call snippets, and board slides. Below is the dossier a credibly-instrumented org would attach for each demo, with specific tools, file conventions, and the numeric texture that makes these feel real rather than toy.
